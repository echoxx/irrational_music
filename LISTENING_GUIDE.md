# Listening Guide: Comparing Pi and E Sonifications

## How to Experiment with Parameters

### Key Parameters and Their Effects

#### 1. **num_digits** (Number of digits to play)
- **Lower (20-40)**: Easier to remember and compare patterns between pi and e
- **Medium (50-100)**: Good balance - patterns emerge without overwhelming
- **Higher (200+)**: Statistical properties become more apparent

**What to listen for:**
- Can you remember the "melody" well enough to compare?
- Do recurring patterns become noticeable with more digits?

#### 2. **note_duration** (Seconds per note)
- **Fast (0.05-0.15s)**: Rhythmic, percussive, patterns blur into "texture"
- **Medium (0.2-0.35s)**: Melodic, individual notes clear, natural pace
- **Slow (0.4-0.8s)**: Contemplative, easy to analyze each transition

**What to listen for:**
- Can you distinguish individual notes?
- Are transitions between notes smooth or choppy?
- Is it too slow to maintain engagement?

#### 3. **crossfade_time** (Overlap between notes)
- **Short (0.01-0.03s)**: More staccato, distinct notes, rhythmic
- **Medium (0.05-0.08s)**: Smooth but clear articulation
- **Long (0.1-0.2s)**: Very smooth, almost organ-like, notes blend

**What to listen for:**
- Do you hear individual notes or a continuous tone?
- Does it sound musical vs mechanical?

### Recommended Experimental Approach

#### Phase 1: Find Your Optimal Note Duration
1. Start with **Preset 1 (Melodic)** - 30 digits, 0.35s/note
2. Try **Preset 2 (Conversational)** - 50 digits, 0.25s/note
3. Try **Preset 4 (Contemplative)** - 40 digits, 0.6s/note

**Goal**: Find the speed where you can clearly hear AND remember patterns

#### Phase 2: Adjust Length
With your preferred note_duration from Phase 1:
- Try fewer digits (20-30) if sequences felt too long
- Try more digits (60-100) if you want more data

**Goal**: Length where you can compare pi vs e without forgetting the first

#### Phase 3: Fine-tune Crossfade
With your preferred duration and length:
- Try crossfade = note_duration × 0.2 (distinct notes)
- Try crossfade = note_duration × 0.3 (balanced)
- Try crossfade = note_duration × 0.5 (smooth flow)

**Goal**: Find the smoothness that sounds most musical to you

### What Makes Pi and E Sound Different?

#### Pi (3.14159...):
- More "random" distribution of digits
- Less predictable patterns
- Feels more varied, less repetitive
- Higher entropy (information content)

#### E (2.71828...):
- Starts with "182818..." - immediate repetition
- Has more local patterns and clusters
- May sound slightly more "ordered"
- Notable sequences: many 2s, 7s, and 8s early on

### Suggested Experiments

1. **"Melody Memory" Test**
   - Preset 1 or 2, only 20 digits
   - Can you hum pi vs e after hearing them?

2. **"Texture Recognition" Test**
   - Preset 3 or 5, 100-200 digits
   - Do they have different "feels" or "colors"?

3. **"Statistical Pattern" Test**
   - Preset 3, 150 digits
   - Does one feel more "jumpy" than the other?
   - Does one stay in certain frequency ranges more?

4. **"Contemplative Analysis" Test**
   - Preset 4, 40 digits
   - Focus on interval sizes (jumps between notes)
   - Which has bigger jumps on average?

### Pro Tips

1. **Close your eyes** while listening to focus on the audio patterns
2. **Listen multiple times** - first for pi, then for e, then compare
3. **Take notes** on what you hear (repeating patterns, overall feel, etc.)
4. **Try different times of day** - you may notice different things when fresh vs tired
5. **Adjust volume** - sometimes lower volume helps focus on patterns vs individual notes

### Scientific Notes

The difference between pi and e's digit sequences is subtle:
- Both appear statistically random in the long run
- Neither has been proven to be "normal" (all digits equally likely)
- Short sequences may show patterns that disappear in longer sequences
- Human pattern recognition is better than random, but also prone to false positives

**The sweet spot** is usually:
- 30-50 digits (enough data, not too much)
- 0.25-0.35s per note (natural listening pace)
- 0.06-0.1s crossfade (smooth but articulate)
- 3-4s pause (time to reset mental "buffer")
